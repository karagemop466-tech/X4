How to read this

Every layer of this desk invented at least one word of its own, and several of them mean something narrower than their everyday sense - unresolved is not unknown, derived is not verified, and signal deliberately excludes the stake. The definitions below are the ones the pages actually use. Each row links to the page where the term is in force, so the definition can be checked against the thing it describes rather than taken on trust.

A term appears here only if a page uses it. A defined word that appears nowhere is noise, so the list is generated from the vocabulary of the site and not from a thesaurus.

TermWhat it means hereWhere it is used
VerifiedRead directly off an official primary source - nfl.com, operations.nfl.com, or a club's own site - and checked against that source. A verified cell carries the URL it came from in the same row.Source register
DerivedComputed from verified inputs by a rule that is written down and re-runnable. A play rate, a snap share and a difference between two lines are derived. Derived is weaker than verified and is always labelled as derived.Methodology
Unverified / blankThe value is not published. A cell the official source does not print is left blank with the reason it is blank, never filled from memory, from another week, or from a non-official aggregator. Every blank is on the flags page with its source link.Flagged for review
FlaggedA value or a document fault that is published *as a flag* rather than corrected - a venue two official sources name differently, a PDF conversion that dropped a column, a referee a game book misprints. Flagged items do not block publication; they are listed with the source that produced them.Flagged for review
Resolved / unresolvedA designation is *resolved* when the player is found in his own game's Game Book - he played, or he is listed as not active or did not play. It is *unresolved* when he is in none of the four sections, which is what happens to a player on a reserve list. Unresolved is never counted as either outcome.Game books
CaptureA verbatim copy of an official page or PDF, stored under data/ops/raw/ with a UTC stamp and a SHA-256. The hash is what makes a later edit detectable rather than invisible.Game Book operations
Reuse gateThe rule that a stored capture is evidence, not content: its text may be re-read and re-checked but is not re-published as a table of its own. Only values derived from it reach the site.Game Book operations
Game Book
TermWhat it means hereWhere it is used
Game BookThe official PDF the league publishes after each game, holding the lineups, substitutions, did-not-play and not-active lists, the header with stadium, surface, roof and weather, and the per-player playtime percentages.Game books
LineupsThe Game Book section listing the eleven players who started on each unit for each club. This is the league's own record of who started, which is not the same thing as a club depth chart.Game books
SubstitutionsThe Game Book's list of players who entered the game without starting. Together with Lineups it is the only official evidence that a player played.Game books
Did Not PlayThe Game Book's list of players who dressed but did not take a snap. Distinct from Not Active, and published as its own section.Game books
Not ActiveThe club's declared game-day inactives. A player here was not eligible to play, which is a stronger statement than Did Not Play and is kept separate.Game books
Playtime percentageThe share of a club's snaps a player took, printed per player at the foot of the Game Book. It is the only official source of playing time, and therefore the fallback for depth where a club publishes no ordered chart.Houston depth
Availability
TermWhat it means hereWhere it is used
DesignationThe game-status label on the official injury report: Out, Doubtful or Questionable. The calibration page measures what each one actually meant rather than assuming it.Game books
Practice statusDNP (did not practise), LP (limited) or FP (full), as printed per day on the official injury report. Reported separately from the designation, because the two are different claims.Injuries & depth
Reserve listA roster status - injured reserve, physically unable to perform, and similar - that removes a player from the game-day roster entirely. A player on a reserve list appears in no Game Book section, which is why his designation is unresolved.Game books
Depth-1 / depth-2 / depth-3A player's ordinal position on his club's own published depth chart. Taken from the club's chart or from measured playing time - never from jersey number, salary or experience, which the brief rejects.Depth & deficiency
Replacement depthHow far down the chart a club must go when a starter is out, and whether a replacement is named at all. It is measured, not assumed: a chain with no named replacement is published as a gap.Replacement chains
DeficiencyThe drop in expected performance when a depth-2 or depth-3 player replaces a starter. Only computed where the replacement is named by an official source and the drop is measurable from official numbers.Matchup matrix
Origination
TermWhat it means hereWhere it is used
OriginationProducing our own number rather than taking a bookmaker's. The order is fixed - side first, then total, then moneyline - so a total cannot be reverse-engineered from a side.Line origination
Fair lineOur own estimate of the correct number for a market.Model lines
Market lineThe price actually offered, captured with a timestamp. Published beside the fair line as its own column, never blended with it.Price store
DifferenceFair line minus market line. Its own column, because an edge that is not separated from the two numbers that produce it cannot be audited.Model lines
Point-in-time (PIT)A price stored with the moment it was seen, and never overwritten. A backtest that uses a price that was not available at the time is testing a strategy that could not have been run.Price store
Closing line value (CLV)The difference between the price taken and the closing price. Used as evidence about process, not as a profit claim.Strategy lab
Strategies
TermWhat it means hereWhere it is used
SignalThe part of a strategy that decides *what* to bet. Scored without seeing any stake, so a lucky staking run cannot make a bad signal look good.Signal vs staking
StakingThe part of a strategy that decides *how much*. Scored apart from the signal for the same reason.Signal vs staking
GateA numeric test a strategy must pass before it is published - sample size, stability, independence, and so on. A gate that can be hand-edited to admit a result is not a gate.Gates & demotion
Demotion gateThe rule that sends a live strategy back to paper when its out-of-sample record degrades. It is the counterpart to the entry gates: without it a strategy that stops working keeps its rank.Gates & demotion
Walk-forwardFit on one window, test on the next, move the window forward. A strategy scored on the data it was fitted to has not been tested.Method lab
Pre-registrationWriting the rule, the sample and the pass criterion down before the result is known, so the criterion cannot be moved to accommodate the result.Method lab
Statistics
TermWhat it means hereWhere it is used
Wilson score intervalA confidence interval for a proportion that stays inside 0 and 1 and behaves sensibly at small n. Used for every play rate, because at n=6 a normal interval produces nonsense.Game books
nThe number of observations behind an estimate. Printed next to every rate and every correlation, because a rate without n is not a measurement.Correlations
Brier score / skillA proper scoring rule: it rewards honest probabilities rather than confident ones, and it cannot be gamed by saying 100% when unsure. Skill is the score relative to a stated baseline.Removal of luck
Game-cluster intervalAn interval built by resampling whole games rather than individual bets. Bets inside one game share a result, so treating them as independent makes an interval far too narrow.Removal of luck
Multiplicity adjustmentThe correction applied when many strategies are tested at once. With enough candidates, the best-looking one will look good by chance.Removal of luck
Joint-evidence gateThe rule that a parlay or prop combination cannot be priced by multiplying its legs' individual probabilities. Legs of the same game are dependent, so the product is not the probability of the combination.Gates & demotion
Correlation (with n)Computed only from officially sourced numbers and published with the number of pairs it rests on and an interval. A correlation without n and an interval is a decoration.Correlations
ROI / win rateDeliberately *not* the ranking statistic. Both are dominated by variance over the sample sizes available here, so ranking on them ranks luck.Leaderboard
