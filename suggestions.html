08 · Review before building

Suggestions

The best path has to be chosen before any of it is implemented. Each proposal below states what it would do, why it matters, how you would know it worked, and the decision it needs from you first. None of them is presented as already built.

Choose before implementing
P0 · Data

Archive a dated snapshot of every official page

Store the standings page, the weekly injury report and the weekly schedule on every fetch, with retrieval time, content hash and the source URL. Rebuild the site from the archive rather than from a live page.

Why: Right now every table is a snapshot of a single moment. Without an archive we cannot prove what was known at decision time, and the significance gate can never be passed retrospectively.

How to verify it worked: Re-running the build from an archived date reproduces byte-identical tables for that date.

Effort: Low · Impact: High

Decision needed before implementing: Agree the retention window and the fetch schedule.

P0 · Data

Repeat the depth capture every week and archive it, then add the official game books for snaps

The depth half of this suggestion is now delivered for one week: tools/depth.py transcribes all 31 clubs that publish an ordered chart (1,558 positions) and joins them to the official injury report, on the Depth &amp; deficiency page. The remaining work is (a) run that same capture every week and keep every snapshot with its retrieval date, and (b) ingest the official game books so that snap counts and the players who actually took the first snap are recorded.

Why: A single-week snapshot is the right shape for originating next week's line and the wrong shape for backtesting: a Week-w forecast may only see what was known before Week w, so an availability term cannot enter the walk-forward model until the capture has been running for a stretch of weeks and been archived. The game books close the second half - a published chart is not proof of who started (F-21) - and they are what turns an absence into a measured point value.

How to verify it worked: An availability term can be added to a Week-w forecast using only snapshots dated before Week w, and a starter can be confirmed from the game book rather than from the pre-game chart.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Very high

Decision needed before implementing: Confirm the game-book format is acceptable to parse and redistribute, and commit to the weekly capture schedule plus the retention window.

P0 · Market

Decide the market strategy before building any pricing

Either authorise a licensed odds feed, or state formally that the project prices its own lines and never compares them to a market. The current code deliberately contains no odds path.

Why: Betting value, bankroll sizing, parlay pricing and player-prop economics are all undefined without prices. Leaving the decision open silently produces numbers that look like conclusions but are not.

How to verify it worked: Either a signed feed agreement, or a published scope statement that removes every value claim from the site.

Effort: Low · Impact: Very high

Decision needed before implementing: Market-independent research, or licensed market comparison.

P1 · Strategy

Group the leaderboard by distinct signal family

Use the published error-correlation matrix to collapse near-duplicate strategies into families before ranking, and label each entry with its family instead of treating every model as an independent competitor.

Why: The correlation matrix shows several strategies with an error correlation of 1.00 - they are the same signal expressed twice. A leaderboard that treats them as rivals overstates how much independent skill exists.

How to verify it worked: Every family leader is separated from every other family leader by a correlation below the agreed threshold.

Effort: Low · Impact: High

Decision needed before implementing: The correlation value at which two strategies count as the same signal.

P1 · Presentation

Publish a band, not a number, for every model line

Show the full strategy range and the implied probability distribution next to each line, and label every model figure as an estimate derived from official results.

Why: A single spread reads as a fact. The range across the nine strategies is the honest representation of what the current data supports, and it shows the reviewer exactly where the model is confident and where it is guessing.

How to verify it worked: No page shows a bare model number without its range and its derivation note.

Effort: Low · Impact: Medium

Decision needed before implementing: Whether the band should be the strategy range or a bootstrap percentile interval.

P1 · Strategy

Run a shadow leaderboard of locked, pre-game predictions

Publish every model line before kickoff with a timestamp, then score it afterwards. No real money, no changes after the fact.

Why: Backtesting on nine weeks of one season is the weakest evidence available. Prospective, timestamped predictions are what turns 'no strategy passes the significance gate' into an actual decision, and they are the only fair basis for a reward contest.

How to verify it worked: After 50 locked games the paired bootstrap interval can be recomputed on prospective data alone.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Very high

Decision needed before implementing: Who locks the predictions, and how is the lock enforced so a line cannot be revised after seeing the market.

P1 · Strategy

Add model-disagreement and absence-impact watchlists

Flag games where the strategies disagree by more than a set threshold, and attach the availability summary for both teams, so extra research effort is aimed at the games that are actually uncertain.

Why: Most games are not close calls. The value of more information is concentrated in a small number of games, and the current site shows the lines but not where uncertainty lives.

How to verify it worked: A weekly shortlist of flagged games, each with the official injury link and the disagreement range.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Medium

Decision needed before implementing: The disagreement threshold that makes a game worth extra research.

P1 · Parlays

Price combinations from a joint model, never from marginals

For any parlay or prop combination, estimate the joint distribution and compare it to the offered payout and to the same legs taken singly. A correlation of 1.00 between two legs means the combination is one bet priced twice.

Why: The correlation matrix already shows that several model legs are near-perfectly collinear. Multiplying marginals in that situation overstates the probability of the parlay winning and is the most common way a strategy loses money for reasons that have nothing to do with skill.

How to verify it worked: Every published combination carries a joint estimate, an explicit dependence assumption and a comparison against the singles.

Effort: High · Impact: High

Decision needed before implementing: Which combination types are eligible for the contest at all.

P2 · Quality

Make data integrity a failing build, not a report

Wire tools/verify.py into continuous integration so that any edit which breaks agreement with the official standings totals fails the build before it can be published.

Why: The verification in this session caught a genuine transcription error and a swapped score. That check should never be optional.

How to verify it worked: A deliberately corrupted row makes the pipeline fail with a named team and the expected versus official totals.

Effort: Low · Impact: High

Decision needed before implementing: Whether the committed standings snapshot is refreshed automatically or manually reviewed.

P2 · Presentation

Add a per-team availability timeline

Show a week-by-week timeline of each team's absence count and unit hit, so a three-week absence is visible at a glance instead of only in the current week's report.

Why: A single report snapshot hides exactly the pattern that matters most for a season-long model: a slowly degrading roster.

How to verify it worked: A timeline view for every team, sourced to the weekly official report.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Medium

Decision needed before implementing: How many weeks of history to retain and how absences that resolve are displayed.

P2 · Presentation

Give every page its own visible flag list

Show the flags that apply to the page you are reading, next to the content, rather than only on the review page.

Why: A reader who lands on the injury page should not have to visit another page to learn that the public report omits players on injured reserve.

How to verify it worked: Each page carries an inline panel listing the flags that constrain it.

Effort: Low · Impact: Medium

Decision needed before implementing: Whether the inline panel is collapsed by default for casual readers.

P3 · Quality

Re-run the strategy search each week and publish the changelog

Recompute the leaderboard weekly from the archive and publish what changed, including strategies that fell out of contention.

Why: Early-season rankings are unstable - the current spread between strategies is smaller than its own confidence interval. Publishing the movement is more honest than publishing a static ranking that looks settled.

How to verify it worked: A dated weekly changelog showing the movement of each strategy's improvement and interval.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Medium

Decision needed before implementing: Whether the leaderboard is frozen for a season or updated continuously.

P0 · Validation

S-15 - Decide whether the significance gate measures mean error or paired win rate

The same 105 out-of-sample games give two different answers. On the bootstrap of the mean absolute-error improvement, the 95% Bonferroni-adjusted interval is [-0.21, +1.82] points and contains zero, so every strategy fails G4. On a paired sign test, S1's spread lands closer to the truth than the league-constant benchmark in 68 of 105 games (64.8%), exact binomial p = 0.0016, which would clear the Bonferroni threshold of 0.00625. Both statistics are computed from the same predictions and both are correct.

Why: The gate currently asks 'is the average improvement distinguishable from zero'. The blowouts dominate that average, and no team-rate model can price a blowout. The sign test asks 'how often is this model better head-to-head', which is the question a staker and a reviewer both actually care about. Choosing one over the other changes whether the leaderboard can ever promote anything - and choosing it now, after seeing that one answer passes, is exactly what a pre-registered gate is supposed to prevent unless the choice is made in the open and recorded before the next evaluation window.

How to verify it worked: Both statistics are published together on gates.html; the gate definition is committed before the next evaluation window; after 50 prospective locked games the two agree or the divergence is explained.

Effort: Low · Impact: Very high

Decision needed before implementing: Define G4 on the mean error difference (current), on the paired win rate (proposed), or on both with a stated primary.

P0 · Data

S-16 - Capture the closing line at kickoff, automatically, or lose CLV permanently

Take a dated snapshot of every market at or after each kickoff, store it under the same snapshot key as the opening and current rows, and recompute closing-line value from it. The store built this session already has the closing slot registered for all 13 games; it is empty only because no game has kicked off yet.

Why: Closing-line value is the only economic measure a staking strategy cannot fake with a lucky run, and it is the only gate input that does not depend on the result at all. A closing line that was not captured at kickoff cannot be reconstructed afterwards from an official source, because there is no official source - so every missed kickoff is a permanently lost observation, not a delay.

How to verify it worked: Every final game has a closing row with a value and a retrieval timestamp at or after kickoff, and CLV recomputes for that game.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Very high

Decision needed before implementing: Who or what captures the snapshot, and at what clock offset from kickoff.

P1 · Presentation

S-17 - Make the signal/staking split load-bearing, or label it decorative

Either introduce a staking rule that is not a monotone function of the hit rate (variable stake sizing, or staking only a selected subset of games), or state on the leaderboard that the two columns are expected to agree under the current flat-stake policy and are published as a cross-check rather than as independent evidence.

Why: Under a flat 1-unit stake at -110, bankroll growth is a pure function of the hit rate, so the staking rank cannot diverge from the signal rank in any informative way. A split that is presented as independent protection while being mathematically collinear with the score it is meant to check gives a false sense of two-factor validation. Publishing the condition under which the split becomes informative is more honest and costs nothing.

How to verify it worked: The leaderboard states the condition under which the ranks can diverge, and either the staking policy produces divergence or the collinearity is stated on the page.

Effort: Low · Impact: Medium

Decision needed before implementing: Whether variable staking is in scope, and if so under what published rule.

How to review them

Ordering rule

Work down this list. If a proposal cannot answer its question, the later ones are not worth evaluating yet.

StepQuestion to answerRule appliedOfficial reference
1Name the decision in one sentence.If the decision cannot be written in a sentence, no data or model is worth building yet.Reference ↗
2What is the smallest data set that could change that decision?Prefer the narrowest official source that can overturn the current answer.Reference ↗
3Is the source official, machine-readable and re-fetchable?Official beats secondary. Re-fetchable beats one-off. A source that cannot be re-read cannot be audited.Reference ↗
4How much would we trust the result if it were reversed?If a reversal would not change the decision, it is not worth the effort.Reference ↗
5What metric and threshold decide success, before we measure?Pre-register the metric and the threshold; choosing them after seeing the result is how luck gets mistaken for skill.Reference ↗
