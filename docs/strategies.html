Why the parlay and prop rows are weaker than they look

Single-bet strategies need a price and an outcome. Parlays and prop combinations need something else as well: the correlation between their legs. Without it a parlay cannot be priced correctly, and a mispriced parlay looks profitable in a backtest for entirely the wrong reason. Gate G6 exists to fail these strategies closed until a correlation is estimated with a published window and standard error.

The prop rows have a second weakness: three of the four depend on knowing whether the replacement player is a starter or a backup, and no official source labels depth strings (F-03). Their entry rules are written so the trigger is the official game status, which is available — but the "and the depth behind him is thin" clause cannot yet be evaluated.

What would move a row from Proposed to Backtested

  1. A licensed play-by-play and price-history dataset (Q-09, Q-10, Q-11).
  2. A bet-log schema that captures the price at strike and the closing price (S-11).
  3. An implemented, frozen model version, so the result is reproducible (S-21 / Q-26).
  4. All gates G0–G8 run and recorded against that model version.

Until item 1 exists, item 4 is impossible. That is why the count above is honest about being zero rather than showing a flattering partial number.
