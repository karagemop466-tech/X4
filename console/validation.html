Validation Gates

Every strategy must pass all nine gates before it is trusted. The gates exist to separate skill from luck — the brief's "remove luck from the equation".

Read the distinction carefully. The statistical techniques below are established and cited. The thresholds are this project's policy choices — they are labelled design, not verified, because a threshold is a decision, not a fact. Most thresholds are deliberately still blank: they must be set from the real dataset size (Q-10, Q-16) rather than picked to look good.

Gates G0 – G8

The statistics that remove luck

Profit is the slowest possible way to tell skill from luck. Closing-line value is far faster, and the distribution test is what actually answers the question "could chance have done this?".

Why closing-line value carries the most weight

The argument, from the cited practitioner analysis: if you possess information the market does not, the odds should shorten after you bet. The closing line is the market's best estimate, so systematically beating it means you knew something — or the book shortened against you because it recognised you as better informed.

  • In a worked example of 1,214 bets with an average edge of 2.19%, the result sat roughly 18.5 standard deviations from chance on CLV. The same record's raw profit, tested alone, could not rule out luck.
  • Practitioner guidance is that a bettor must beat the fair closing price about 70% of the time to have any positive expected value at all.
  • Even after 1,000 bets there was roughly a 1-in-10 chance that luck alone produced a 2% profit where the expectation was a 2% loss.
  • Dispersion scales with the odds: the reported standard deviation of the opening-to-closing price ratio was about 0.043 at odds of 1.25 and about 0.14 at odds of 5.0 — so a single fixed CLV threshold would be wrong for longshots.

These figures come from soccer odds populations, not NFL, and from a practitioner source rather than a peer-reviewed one. They are used here to indicate the order of magnitude of evidence required, not as NFL parameters.

Gate failure policy

  • G0 fails → the run is void. Do not report the number, do not debug around it.
  • G6 fails → the parlay is unpriceable. Fail closed. Never substitute a guessed correlation.
  • G4 fails → reject. Do not fall back to the in-sample result.
  • G5 fails → demote to probation rather than reject; it may be one bad season.
  • G7 fails → cap the strategy at realised capacity, not modelled capacity.
  • G8 fails → reject. An edge smaller than the cost of crossing the spread is not an edge.
Gate specification is this project's own; the statistical techniques rest on the cited sources in the Source column. No strategy has been run through any gate.
