THE LEADERBOARD

Signal is scored. Staking is reported. They are never the same column.

A lucky staking run is indistinguishable from skill on a small sample: twenty winning bets at even money look identical whether the edge is real or the coin fell right. So the leaderboard ranks the signal — whether our number beat the market number — and shows staking beside it as context. A strategy with a brilliant staking record and no signal cannot rank.

WHY THE SPLIT MATTERS

The failure this prevents

A leaderboard scored on profit rewards the strategy that took the most risk during a lucky stretch. Scoring the signal instead means the winner is the strategy whose numbers were closest to the truth, and the staking column is left to do the only job it can do honestly: tell you what the paper record looked like, with the size of the sample printed next to it.
