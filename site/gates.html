13 · Gates

Separate the signal from the staking, then police the door

Three separate defences against mistaking luck for skill. The signal is scored on line accuracy with no results bought; staking is shown beside it and can never lift it; and a single failed gate returns a live strategy to paper immediately, with no averaging and no grace period.

0 of 9 live
Signal

Built only from accuracy

Benchmark improvement (35%), significance (30%), weekly stability (20%) and calibration (15%). No staking result can enter the score.

Staking

Shown, never scored

Flat 1 unit at -110 on every graded call. It is the unit a staker reads, and it is deliberately quarantined from the ranking that decides eligibility.

Demotion

One failure is enough

Live requires every gate. Anything else is paper, which is where all 9 strategies are today - not because the site is unfinished, but because the significance gate has not been passed.

01 · Signal vs staking

Two ranks, on purpose

Both ranks are published for the same strategy. They agree today, and that is the finding rather than a success: under a flat stake at a fixed price, bankroll growth is a monotone function of the hit rate, which the signal score already uses. The split becomes load-bearing once stakes vary with the bankroll or a strategy stakes only a selected subset of games. Published as S-17.

02 · The demotion gate

What sends a live strategy back to paper

Promotion

Every gate, every time

A strategy is Live only when it passes all 7 gates in the same evaluation window. Passing six of seven is paper. There is no partial credit and no conditional promotion.

Demotion

Any single failure, immediately

Once Live, a strategy returns to paper the moment any gate fails in a re-evaluation: the significance interval re-crosses zero, calibration breaks its tolerance, the minimum sample is not met, or a stability share falls below the declared floor. Re-entry requires passing every gate again from scratch.

Retirement

After two demotions

A strategy that has been demoted twice is retired rather than cycled again. A model that keeps failing and passing in alternation is showing that the gate is noisy, not that the model is good.

Why it exists

Because promotion is cheap and demotion is not

Anyone can publish a rule that promotes a hot strategy. The demotion rule is what makes the leaderboard falsifiable: it commits, in advance, to the exact evidence that will take a strategy's eligibility away.
