REPLACEMENT DEPTH

The fact of an absence is official. The depth behind it is not.

An official injury report tells you a starter is out. It does not tell you whether the man replacing him is the second-string player or the fourth. That distinction is the whole point of tracking depth, and no official NFL source labels it: the league's depth-chart address returns HTTP 404, verified below. This page therefore publishes what is official, measures the gap, and refuses to invent a string.

THE GAP

What the official record does and does not say

404 VERIFIED
WHAT IS OFFICIAL

The absence

Player, club, position, injury, practice status and game designation, row by row, from nfl.com/injuries. Every row on this page links to it.

WHAT IS NOT

The string

No official page labels a player second-, third- or fourth-string. Club depth charts, where a club publishes one, are ordered lists without string labels. A guessed string would be a fabricated input.

THE SUBSTITUTE

The transaction date

The closest official proxy is when a club lost the player and what it did next: a reserve-list move, a signing, a waiver claim. A club that signs a replacement within 24 hours is telling you the depth behind the starter was thin. That is measurable from nfl.com/transactions, and it is the depth measure this build uses where the record allows it.

THE HONEST LABEL

Unresolved

Where the record does not allow it, the cell says depth unresolved and the row stays blank. It is not filled with the previous week's value or with a plausible guess.

TRANSACTIONS

The official record that dates a replacement

Every row is a league-published transaction. The date is what turns 'a player is missing' into 'a club had N hours to find a body', which is the depth signal that can actually be measured.
