T30 · real prices, not model estimates

What the market actually said, next to what the desk said

Every number on this page is one of three things, and the page never lets them be confused: a captured price from one licensed book published by ESPN, an official NFL.com result with the game page that proves it, or a model estimate labelled with the strategy that produced it. The NFL publishes no betting lines, so no price here is official, and every price row carries the resource URL it was archived from.

The uncomfortable result: on 16 graded games the desk's spread error is worse than simply taking the closing line's implied margin (best strategy 9.32 points against 8.47), and every strategy's fair numbers are compressed toward zero relative to the board. That is published on this page rather than buried, because a desk that only publishes its wins is not a decision desk.

Origination runs in the required order: the side is priced first, then the total, then the moneyline. Each market shows the desk's fair number, the market number and the difference as three separate values, in that order, never blended. A spread is written as the margin the home team is expected to win by, so both numbers sit on one axis and the subtraction is meaningful: the market margin is the negation of the posted home handicap, and a positive difference means the desk rates home more highly than the board does, so home is the value side. The posted handicap is on the snapshot table below for anyone who wants to read the number that was actually on the board. The moneyline difference is in probability points on the de-vigged close, not on the raw price, so the bookmaker's margin is removed before the comparison. Fair numbers are model estimates, not prices.

The skill board. Ranking uses out-of-sample error only - the model's number against the official final score - and nothing on this board can see a stake. vs market is how many points better than the closing line the model is; a negative value means the closing line was better than the model, which is what a market with more information should look like. Closing-line value is measured the only way that can come out negative: take the side the desk favoured against the opening number, then ask how far the market moved that side's way by the close. Measuring it against the side picked at the close would be a tautology, because that side is defined by the close having already moved. A lucky staking run cannot move a strategy up this table, by construction: no stake is an input.

A diagnostic, published because it is uncomfortable and because a reader can check it from the same data. Compression is the desk's mean absolute handicap divided by the market's: a value below 1 means the desk's number sits closer to zero than the board's, so it takes the underdog side mechanically rather than because it knows something. This is the reason a pick's win rate is not used as evidence of skill anywhere on this site. It is reported here and not corrected: changing a model after seeing the results would be hindsight.
Integrity

Two captures of the same book, taken independently, compared number by number. The comparison is re-derived from the two CSVs by tools/reconcile_prices.py, which is part of the publication gate.
The one difference is a naming difference, and it is flagged. In this archive the word close means the last line the book was showing when the capture was taken, so it is filled for every game, played or not. In the integrity store on the price-snapshot page the same number is called current, and close is reserved for a game that has actually been played. The numbers agree; the words do not. See flag F-38.
What this page does not have

Blank, and why

No prop prices

Player props and prop combos

No official or licensed source for player-level prices was confirmed for this slate, so no prop price, prop line or prop correlation is published. The cells are blank rather than estimated. That is a gap in the data, not a finding about the players.

No order book

Depth is line survival, not liquidity

Depth here is how often the opening number was still the closing number. Real depth - how much money a book will take at a price - needs a level-2 feed this project does not have and will not invent.

One book

No consensus, no arbitrage claim

Prices come from a single book. Nothing here is a market consensus and nothing here can support a claim about arbitrage, which needs two books and both prices.

Small sample

16 games is not a sample, it is a start

Every interval on this page is wide enough to swallow zero. The page reports that instead of rounding it away, and the demotion gate keeps every strategy on paper until the interval says otherwise.
