The correction this page had to make.

The first draft of this dataset published a column called clv_points. It was wrong: it measured how far the model's line sat from the closing number, which is a disagreement, not closing-line value. CLV is the difference between the price taken and the price at the close, and this project has never taken a price. The column is now disagreement_points, the CLV ledger is published with zero rows, and every gate that depends on CLV reports BLOCKED instead of a comfortable-looking number. Closing-line value becomes measurable the first time the capture writes a row before the ball is kicked.

GATE 3 · ORIGINATION ORDER

Side first, then the total, then the moneyline

The order is not a preference here; it is a dependency. The side is the only quantity estimated from team strength. The total is estimated from scoring and conceding rates. The moneyline is not an independent estimate: it is the side converted to a win probability through the same distribution whose spread is measured at 13.47 points from 57 official games. Publishing it as a third independent opinion would be double-counting one signal.

The Baltimore row is the whole argument for this gate.

By the "starter is out" flag alone, Baltimore looks like one more team missing its quarterback. What actually happened is three separate events: the starter went to injured reserve; the backup was already out for the season with turf toe; and an undrafted third-stringer started a game before the club traded for a veteran who had started eight games elsewhere. The replacement is not the second string, and the team that takes the field is not the team the season-to-date model has been rating. That is measurable here because the depth slot and the replacement's own starts are recorded — and it is invisible to a binary flag.

Two official sources, two absence windows. NFL.com reported the injury as 6–9 weeks on 2026-11-08; the coach's statement carried by NFL.com on 2026-11-13 says 6–10 weeks. Both are recorded on the row and neither is chosen. That is flag F-04, and it is why the availability field underneath this table is not fit for pricing until a human rules on it. A depth measurement is only worth what the availability assumption under it is worth.

The parlay-relevant one.

Margin and game total are negatively dependent: r = -0.322 on n = 54, interval [-0.543, -0.060]. A "favourite covers and the game goes over" parlay therefore has two legs that pull against each other, so pricing it as the product of two independent probabilities overstates the payout. This is exactly what the parlay gate exists to catch, and it is the one correlation in this dataset strong enough to act on. The teammate correlation between the two scores is 0.018 on n = 57 - indistinguishable from zero, so "both teams over" legs cannot assume dependence either way.

GATE 4 · SIGNAL vs STAKING

Two separate boards, so a lucky run cannot impersonate skill

The left block asks is there a signal: did the line beat the close, is the probability calibrated, how big is the sample. The right block asks what did the staking do to the bankroll: units, drawdown, survival. They are never added together, because a bankroll number is the product of both and tells you which one you are looking at only when you can see them apart.

One settled game per strategy cannot separate skill from luck, and publishing a hit rate of 1.000 for five of nine strategies would be exactly the "lucky staking run masking a bad signal" failure this requirement names. The Brier values are printed because they are computable, but they are computed on one row and carry no evidence. The gate that matters is the sample-size gate, and it is failing.

Two identical edges, two staking rules, opposite outcomes: at 5,000 bets the fair coin that survives flat staking is busted under proportional staking, and the genuine edge that turns 100 into 178 flat falls to 16.79 proportionally because a drawdown cut the position size at the worst moment. The signal never changed. Only the staking did. A leaderboard that ranks on bankroll is ranking the column on the right and calling it the column on the left.

GATE 8 · DEMOTION

A gate that sends a live strategy back to paper

What "paper" means here.

A demoted strategy keeps publishing its line and its pre-registered bets into the price store and the results feed, but its staking board is frozen and its signal board keeps accumulating. Promotion back requires re-clearing D1 and D2 over a fresh sample. The gate is therefore a loop, not a punishment: the only way out is more out-of-sample evidence, which is the one thing a lucky run cannot manufacture.
